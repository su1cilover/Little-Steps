Understanding

What is actually going on

Depression is an illness, not a weakness

It changes how the brain handles mood, energy, sleep, and thinking. It is common, it is real, and it is treatable. Most people who get support, through talking therapy, medication, or both, feel meaningfully better over time.

Signs it may be depression
  • Feeling sad, empty, or numb most days for two weeks or more
  • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
  • Sleeping much more or much less than usual
  • Constant tiredness, or changes in appetite
  • Trouble concentrating, or feeling worthless or guilty
  • Thoughts of death or of not wanting to be here

Only a trained professional can diagnose depression. This list is not a test.

Suicidal thoughts are more common than people think

Many people have them at some point. Having them does not mean you are broken or that you will act on them. They often signal that the pain has grown bigger than your current ways of coping. They come in waves, and waves pass, especially with support.

Depression tells convincing lies

It says things like "nothing will ever change," "I'm a burden," or "no one cares." These feel like facts, but they are symptoms. Check them with someone you trust.

Small things that help over time
  • Talk to a doctor, psychologist, or counsellor. This is the most effective step.
  • Tell one trusted person how you are really doing.
  • Keep a basic routine: wake up, eat, take any prescribed medicine, get some daylight.
  • Move a little, even a 10-minute walk.
  • Protect your sleep and go easy on alcohol.
  • Make the next task tiny. A glass of water counts.

If you're supporting someone

  • Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking does not plant the idea.
  • Listen without judging or rushing to fix. Stay with them.
  • Help them secure anything they could use to hurt themselves.
  • Help them reach a crisis line or professional, and call emergency services if the danger is immediate.

If you might act on thoughts of ending your life

Reach out to someone now. Call your local emergency number (911 in the Philippines). If someone is with you, tell them. If you can, move away from anything you could use to hurt yourself.
